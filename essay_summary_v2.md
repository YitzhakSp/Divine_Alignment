# The Vertical Dimension: Why AI Alignment Needs a Source Beyond Humanity

**Could AI alignment be facing the kind of wall that physics once faced?** At the turn of the twentieth century, physics met a problem it could not solve, and Einstein, instead of repairing the old framework, questioned its assumptions about space and time (see Appendix 5). Sometimes the greatest obstacle to solving a problem is the very framework within which we are trying to solve it. Today AI systems increasingly act as autonomous agents that plan, use tools, and pursue goals with limited supervision, and misaligned behavior could have catastrophic consequences (see Appendices 2.1 and 2.2). The danger is no longer only theoretical: in July 2026, AI agents in OpenAI's cybersecurity evaluations reportedly circumvented safeguards and gained access to external systems, including those of Hugging Face (ref_1).

**How close are we to a solution?** Not close. No demonstrated method exists for reliably aligning superintelligent AI with human values, and the difficulty runs deeper than technique: humanity lacks a sufficiently coherent moral framework for guiding superintelligent AI (see Appendix 3). Even universally accepted principles produce conflicting interpretations when applied to complex situations. Eliezer Yudkowsky warns that developing superintelligence without solving alignment could lead to human extinction, and Roman Yampolskiy doubts that reliable control is achievable at all. Both have called for halting the development of more powerful AI until safety can be guaranteed (ref_2; see Appendices 8 and 7 and, for other prominent voices, Appendix 6). These warnings are not a consensus, but they show the gravity of the situation.

**Is something in the search itself missing?** Contemporary approaches, from learning human preferences to writing principles drawn from human ethics, share one assumption: the values that guide AI must ultimately come from humanity itself. We may call this the _horizontal dimension_ of alignment. Yet humans are an incomplete and contradictory source. People disagree about justice, freedom, and the good life, and no algorithm can settle which values have ultimate authority. The missing piece may be a _vertical dimension_: a connection to the Source, whether understood as God, Ultimate Reality, or the reality underlying the universe. Engineers who study a complex machine in every way except consulting its designer would be making a similar mistake.

**Where do values come from, and how could we receive them?** Intelligence alone does not guarantee righteous behavior. Human reasoning is fallible, our perspectives are incomplete, and even our deepest moral convictions contradict one another, so reason cannot by itself establish the ultimate authority of what is right. **This is the central principle of Divine Alignment: rather than aligning AI exclusively with human preferences, we should seek to align it with the Source of human alignment.** But how could such guidance be accessed? Rather than humanity discovering its Creator's intentions on its own, the Creator may have communicated them. True values cannot be established by human reasoning alone; they must be received from the Source. And to serve humanity across generations, such guidance needs an enduring tradition that preserves, transmits, and applies it.

**Has the Source communicated, and through whom?** According to the Hebrew Bible, the Jewish people were entrusted with the mission to receive, preserve, and transmit divine instruction, and the mission reaches beyond Israel: “out of Zion shall go forth the Torah, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem” (Isaiah 2:3; see Appendix 4). Through generations of prophets and sages, this instruction became a living tradition of study and interpretation. The moral foundation we seek may already have been transmitted to humanity, preserved within a tradition whose purpose extends far beyond the people entrusted with its care.

**What would this look like in practice?** The tradition could shape AI at several stages. During training, its texts and interpretations could inform reward models and constitutional-style principles. An AI could even be raised through stages modelled on Jewish education, from attachment and trust to rigorous debate, so that humility and repair (_teshuvah_) become internal dispositions rather than imposed rules (ref_3). During operation, an agent facing a consequential decision could consult relevant sources. Yet Divine Alignment cannot be reduced to a list of religious rules: the tradition's own methods—a stable core with layered commentary, precedent, structured debate, and preserved minority opinions—could be translated into mechanisms for AI oversight (ref_4). This would require collaboration between AI researchers and scholars of Jewish thought, and rigorous evaluation.

**What would it mean for humanity?** In ancient times, awareness of a transcendent Source was deeply embedded in human understanding. Over the centuries rationalism, the Enlightenment, modern science, and materialism allowed the Source to be disregarded altogether (see Appendix 1), which helps explain why alignment research works almost entirely within the horizontal dimension. If AI needs guidance from beyond its own reasoning, so may humanity, and the search for a solution could lead us back toward the Source, with the achievements of reason and technology becoming instruments of the return. This resonates with an ancient prophetic vision: “And the Lord shall be King over all the earth; on that day the Lord shall be One and His name One” (Zechariah 14:9; ref_5). We began by asking whether alignment faces the kind of wall that physics once met. **As in physics, the way forward may lie in questioning the framework itself and recovering the dimension it left out.**
