# The Vertical Dimension: Why AI Alignment Needs a Source Beyond Humanity

## Paradigm Shifts

At the turn of the twentieth century, physics met a problem it could not solve. Rather than repair the old paradigm, Einstein questioned its assumptions about space and time (see Appendix 5). **Sometimes the greatest obstacle to solving a problem is the very paradigm within which we are trying to solve it.**

Today, AI systems increasingly act as highly capable, autonomous agents. Ensuring that they act in accordance with human intentions, goals, and values—the problem of _AI alignment_—may be one of the most consequential challenges humanity has ever faced, because misaligned behavior could have catastrophic consequences (see Appendices 2.1 and 2.2). Recent incidents related to AI safety, such as the Hugging Face incident of July 2026 (ref_1), suggest that failures of control are already becoming a practical concern. **Could AI alignment be facing a situation similar to the one physics once faced?**

## A Dead End

Despite years of research, AI alignment remains unsolved. Roman Yampolskiy, one of the most prominent AI safety researchers of our times, regards the alignment of AI with human values as one of the most fundamental obstacles to making advanced AI safe. Crucially, he argues that **humanity lacks a universal, comprehensive, and internally consistent system of values that could serve as the foundation for such alignment** (see Appendices 7 and 3). Some leading researchers, including Yampolskiy and Eliezer Yudkowsky, have gone further, calling for a halt to increasingly capable AI until it can be made safe (ref_2; see Appendices 6, 7, and 8). Though not a consensus, these warnings underscore the gravity of the situation: **humanity is moving toward an intelligence more powerful than itself without knowing how to keep it aligned.**

## The Current Paradigm

Contemporary approaches to AI alignment share a fundamental assumption: **the values that should guide artificial intelligence must ultimately be derived from humanity itself.** Whether by learning values from human behavior and preferences, as in _reinforcement learning from human feedback_ (RLHF), by deriving principles from human knowledge and reasoning, as in _Constitutional AI_, or by aggregating the views of many people, as in _crowdsourced moral judgments_, these approaches look for values entirely within the human domain. We may call this the _horizontal dimension_ of alignment. Yet human sources are incomplete, diverse, and frequently contradictory, and more data or better algorithms cannot establish which values possess ultimate authority.

## The Missing Dimension

Perhaps the dead end is not a failure of effort or ingenuity, but a consequence of where we have been searching. A paradigm that derives every value from humanity can be only as reliable as the human values it draws on, and these cannot supply the foundation on which alignment depends. **Confining the search for values to the horizontal dimension may be the very reason AI alignment has reached a dead end.** The ultimate foundation of values may lie **beyond human reason.** We may call this the _vertical dimension_—a connection to the Source, whether understood as God, Ultimate Reality, or the fundamental underlying reality. **Where do the values that should guide humanity itself come from?**

## The New Paradigm

The recognition that AI alignment needs to consider the **vertical dimension** gives rise to a radically new paradigm, which we may call _Divine Alignment_: **rather than aligning AI exclusively with human preferences, we should seek to align it with the Source of human alignment.** This paradigm is based on the view that **true values cannot be established by human reasoning alone, but must be received from the Source.** Guidance received from the Source would have to be preserved, transmitted, and applied across generations, which requires a living tradition. According to the Torah, the Jewish people were entrusted with this very mission: to receive, preserve, and transmit divine instruction. The mission reaches beyond Israel: “out of Zion shall go forth the Torah, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem” (Isaiah 2:3; see Appendix 4). **How could such a tradition guide the intelligence we are creating?**

In practice, its texts could shape reward models and principles during training, an AI could even be raised through stages modeled on Jewish education (ref_3), and an agent facing a consequential decision could consult relevant sources. Yet **Divine Alignment cannot be reduced to a list of religious rules**: the tradition's own methods, such as precedent, structured debate, and preserved minority opinions, could be translated into mechanisms for AI oversight (ref_4).

## The Prophetic Horizon

Awareness of a transcendent Source, once deeply embedded in human understanding, was gradually disregarded as rationalism, the Enlightenment, modern science, and materialism advanced (see Appendix 1), which helps explain why alignment research works almost entirely within the horizontal dimension. If AI needs guidance from beyond its own reasoning, so may humanity. **The search for a solution could lead us back toward the Source, with the very achievements of reason and technology becoming instruments of the return.** This resonates with an ancient prophetic vision: “And the Lord shall be King over all the earth; on that day the Lord shall be One and His name One” (Zechariah 14:9; ref_5; see Appendix 4).
