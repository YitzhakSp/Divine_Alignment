## Compared with the other outlines

**Strengths**
- A reader who stops after the opening paragraph still gets the claim. It is the best version for sharing, or for readers who won't finish.
- The "wall" section holds all the evidence in one place, so the dead end is stronger than in the question ladder.

**Weaknesses**
- It gives up the suspense of the full essay, where the vertical dimension arrives as a discovery. The reader knows the destination from the first paragraph.
- The opening thesis needs careful wording so it doesn't read as an overclaim before the argument is made. Phrases like "may have reached" and "could turn" must carry that.

**Fit**
- outline_fsum_1.md is the safest compression.
- outline_fsum_2.md (question ladder) is the most faithful to the essay's dynamics.
- This one is the most efficient for quick reading.

## Shared with the other outlines

- All three drop ref_2, ref_5, the *Aleinu* sentence and the Copernicus analogy.
- All three leave the same open decisions: whether to renumber the references 1 to 5 or keep the old numbers (ref_1, 3, 4, 6, 7), and whether to keep the appendix pointers in the text or replace them with one closing line.
- All nine appendices stay cited. In this outline Appendix 4 appears once (part 3), and Appendices 2.1 and 2.2 are cited once.
