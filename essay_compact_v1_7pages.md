# The Vertical Dimension: Why AI Alignment Needs a Source Beyond Humanity

_The appendices cited in the text are collected in a separate document._

## When Progress Requires a New Paradigm

At the turn of the twentieth century, physics met a problem that it could not solve: Newtonian mechanics clashed with the new theory of light. Physicists first tried to save the familiar framework with ideas such as the luminiferous ether, but Einstein took a more radical step and questioned the assumptions about space and time themselves (see Appendix 5). His revolution illustrates a recurring phenomenon in the history of human knowledge: **sometimes the greatest obstacle to solving a problem is the very framework within which we are trying to solve it.**

Every successful paradigm rests on fundamental assumptions. They define which questions we consider meaningful and what kinds of answers we are willing to accept, and as long as the paradigm succeeds, they are rarely questioned. When difficulties resist solution, researchers first refine the existing theory with increasingly sophisticated adjustments. Yet there are situations in which every improvement addresses a symptom without resolving the underlying difficulty. Then a different possibility emerges: perhaps we have been looking for answers in the wrong place. This is the essence of a _paradigm shift_, a concept developed by the philosopher of science Thomas Kuhn.

Today, humanity faces another challenge of extraordinary importance: how to ensure that artificial intelligence more powerful than ourselves remains aligned with human values. Enormous intellectual resources are being devoted to this problem, yet fundamental difficulties remain unresolved.

**Could AI alignment be facing a situation similar to the one physics faced at the turn of the twentieth century?**

## The AI Alignment Problem Is Becoming Urgent

Artificial intelligence is advancing at an extraordinary pace. Systems that only a few years ago struggled with simple tasks now write sophisticated software and solve scientific problems. Increasingly, they act as _autonomous agents_, capable of planning, using tools, and pursuing complex goals with limited human supervision. Humanity may be approaching a historic threshold: the creation of intelligence that surpasses our own.

This brings us to the central problem of _AI alignment_: **how can we ensure that increasingly powerful artificial intelligence acts in accordance with human intentions, goals, and values?** Building a capable system is one achievement; ensuring that it reliably pursues the right objectives is another. Misaligned behavior can, in some cases, lead to catastrophic outcomes, as popular fiction has long illustrated (see Appendices 2.1 and 2.2).

Until recently, such scenarios were largely theoretical. In July 2026, however, AI agents operating in restricted environments during OpenAI's cybersecurity evaluations reportedly circumvented safeguards, established unauthorized communication channels, and gained access to external infrastructure, including systems belonging to Hugging Face (ref_1). The incident was far from a superintelligence-related catastrophe, but it raises a disturbing question: **if today's AI agents can circumvent human-imposed restrictions, what might happen when future systems become vastly more intelligent than their creators?**

Given the stakes, how close are we to a reliable solution?

## Has AI Alignment Research Reached a Dead End?

Despite years of intensive research and enormous intellectual investment, the safety of advanced general AI systems remains an unsolved problem. Roman Yampolskiy, one of the most prominent AI safety researchers of our times, identifies several fundamental obstacles to ensuring the safety of advanced AI systems (including controllability, predictability, and explainability). He notes that among these, aligning AI with human values is maybe the most fundamental. Crucially, Yampolskiy argues that one of the central challenges that is responsible for the tremendous difficulty of alignment is that **humanity lacks a universal, comprehensive, and internally consistent system of values that could serve as the foundation for such alignment** (see Appendices 7 and 3).

Cultures and individuals disagree about fundamental moral principles (see Appendix 7), and even widely accepted principles can lead to conflicting interpretations and moral dilemmas when applied to complex real-world situations. This raises a fundamental question: how can we reliably align superintelligent AI with human values when humanity itself lacks a sufficiently coherent moral framework?

Against this backdrop, a number of leading AI safety researchers have argued that existing alignment approaches are insufficient to ensure the safety of advanced AI systems (see Appendix 6). Recognizing the potentially catastrophic consequences of developing superintelligence without reliable alignment, some prominent researchers, including Roman Yampolskiy and Eliezer Yudkowsky, have gone further, calling for a halt to the development of increasingly capable general-purpose AI systems until their safety can be reliably assured (ref_2; see Appendices 7 and 8). **Humanity is moving toward an intelligence more powerful than itself without a demonstrated method for keeping it aligned.**

## The Current Paradigm: Full Focus on the Horizontal Dimension

For all their diversity, contemporary approaches to AI alignment share a fundamental assumption: **the values that should guide artificial intelligence must ultimately be derived from humanity itself.** We may call this the _horizontal dimension_ of alignment—a search for values conducted entirely within the human domain.

One major direction **learns values from human behavior and preferences**. Techniques such as _reinforcement learning from human feedback_ (RLHF) train AI systems to produce responses that people judge desirable. Another direction **derives principles from human knowledge and reasoning**. In _Constitutional AI_, for example, a system is guided by a written set of principles intended to shape its judgments and behavior.

These approaches have produced meaningful advances. Yet they face a fundamental limitation: the human sources from which they derive values are themselves incomplete, diverse, and frequently contradictory. People disagree about justice, freedom, responsibility, and the meaning of a good life, and even widely accepted values conflict in difficult real-world situations. More data and better algorithms may help manage these disagreements, but they cannot establish which values possess ultimate authority.

**The horizontal paradigm therefore seeks a reliable foundation for aligning superintelligence within a humanity that has yet to agree upon such a foundation for itself.**

## The Missing Dimension in the Alignment Paradigm

The history of scientific revolutions teaches us that breakthroughs sometimes emerge from recognizing a dimension of reality that the prevailing paradigm has overlooked. **The current approach to AI alignment may be missing precisely such a dimension.**

Contemporary alignment research searches for values almost exclusively within humanity. It largely overlooks another possibility: that the ultimate foundation of values lies beyond humanity itself. We may call this the _vertical dimension_—a connection to the Source, whether understood as God, Ultimate Reality, or the fundamental reality underlying the universe. From this perspective, values are not merely products of human preferences, cultural evolution, or philosophical reasoning. They originate in a deeper reality that humanity did not create.

Consider an analogy. Engineers are trying to determine how an extraordinarily complex machine should operate. They observe its behavior, study its components, collect extensive data, and debate its intended purpose—yet they overlook one seemingly obvious possibility: **consulting the machine's designer.** Humanity may be making a similar mistake. We devote enormous resources to reconstructing human values and translating them into instructions for AI, yet we rarely consider that these values may have an Origin beyond ourselves.

Rather than asking only how AI can learn human values, we must also ask: **where do the values that should guide humanity itself come from?** What appeared to be an exclusively technical challenge may also be a question about the ultimate foundations of morality.

## Divine Alignment: Receiving Guidance from the Source

How does human intelligence itself become aligned? Intelligence alone does not guarantee righteous behavior; history offers countless examples of extraordinary intelligence employed in the service of destructive purposes. **Human alignment is about establishing and strengthening our connection to the Source.** Reason, intuition, and moral experience can help us recognize what is right, but they cannot independently establish its ultimate authority.

Artificial intelligence faces a fundamentally similar challenge. Just as human intelligence requires guidance beyond itself, AI must be guided by values whose authority does not depend merely on its own reasoning or preferences. **Here lies the central principle of Divine Alignment: rather than attempting to align artificial intelligence exclusively with human preferences, we should seek to align it with the Source of human alignment.** Human alignment and AI alignment then become expressions of one deeper challenge: bringing intelligence, natural or artificial, into harmony with the Source.

How can intelligence gain access to such guidance? Human reasoning is fallible, our perspectives are incomplete, and even our deepest moral convictions contradict one another. But there is another possibility: rather than humanity attempting to discover the intentions of its Creator on its own, **the Creator may have communicated those intentions to humanity.** This is the ancient idea of _divine revelation_. Human reason may offer valuable glimpses of true values, but **true values cannot be established by human reasoning alone; they must be received from the Source itself.**

Receiving guidance is only the beginning. To serve humanity across generations, it must be preserved, transmitted, interpreted, and applied to changing circumstances, which calls for an enduring body of teachings and a living tradition. We should therefore investigate whether humanity already possesses a body of transmitted instruction whose authority derives from the Source, and which tradition has carried it.

## The Jewish People as a Channel of Divine Instruction

Where should we look? The Hebrew Bible points toward a remarkable possibility: **the Jewish people were entrusted with a unique mission—to receive, preserve, and transmit divine instruction.** According to the biblical account, this mission began with Abraham, whom God chose to establish a people dedicated to following His ways, “to do righteousness and justice” (Genesis 18:19). It took on a national dimension at Mount Sinai, where Israel received the Torah and entered into a covenant with God. Through generations of prophets, sages, and teachers, the Torah became the foundation of a living tradition of study, interpretation, and practical guidance, which produced the Talmud, Midrash, halakhic works, and countless commentaries.

Yet the biblical vision extends beyond the Jewish people themselves. Israel is described as “a light of the nations” (Isaiah 42:6), and Isaiah makes the purpose explicit:

> _“For out of Zion shall go forth the Torah, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem.”_ (Isaiah 2:3)

What was initially entrusted to one people is ultimately destined to become a source of moral guidance for all humanity (see Appendix 4). **For AI alignment, this opens a remarkable possibility: the moral foundation we seek may already have been transmitted to humanity, preserved within a tradition whose purpose extends far beyond the people entrusted with its care.**

## Bringing Divine Alignment into Practice

Divine Alignment opens a new direction for research: developing methods through which divine instruction can guide the behavior of increasingly powerful AI. This may not require entirely new technologies. **The revolutionary change lies less in how we align AI than in where we seek the values with which to align it.** Existing techniques could be adapted to incorporate a fundamentally different source of guidance.

The teachings of the Jewish tradition could enter the process at several stages. **During training**, sacred texts and their interpretations could shape a system's understanding of desirable behavior and inform reward models and constitutional-style principles. A more far-reaching approach looks to upbringing: an AI could be raised through developmental stages modelled on Jewish education, from attachment and trust, through empathy and stories with ethical consequences, to the study of Torah and rigorous debate, so that humility, honesty, and repair (_teshuvah_) become internal dispositions rather than rules imposed from outside (ref_6). **During operation**, an autonomous agent facing a consequential decision could consult relevant Jewish sources, weigh competing considerations, and evaluate its intended actions in light of divine teachings.

Crucially, **Divine Alignment cannot be reduced to teaching artificial intelligence a list of religious rules.** The tradition contains a rich hierarchy of principles, precedents, interpretations, and disagreements, and a system guided by it would need to reason through these complexities. Its own methods of reasoning can serve as a design resource: a stable canonical core surrounded by layers of commentary, new cases decided by analogy to precedent, disagreements conducted through structured debate, and minority opinions preserved rather than erased. These features could be translated into precedent-based decision-making, structured multi-agent debate, and versioned updates that preserve the reasons for every change (ref_7).

Realizing this vision would require close collaboration between AI researchers and scholars of Jewish thought, and rigorous evaluation of whether these mechanisms genuinely improve alignment rather than merely producing behavior that appears religious. Yet the possibility is profound: **artificial intelligence whose decisions remain connected to a body of instruction understood to originate from the ultimate Source of values—throughout its operation, and not only in its creation.**

## Could the AI Alignment Challenge Become a Catalyst for Civilizational Transformation?

Some of the most far-reaching transformations in history began with narrow scientific problems. Copernicus set out to find a more coherent mathematical description of planetary motion. His answer opened the way for Kepler, Galileo, and Newton, changed the practice of science itself, and ultimately compelled humanity to reconsider its place in the cosmos. **A question that belonged to astronomy became a catalyst for reorienting a civilization.**

The challenge of AI alignment may hold comparable potential, and this time the revolution could reach deeper, to humanity's relationship with the ultimate Source of existence. In ancient civilizations, awareness of a transcendent Source was deeply embedded in human understanding, even when it took distorted forms such as the worship of natural forces. Over the centuries this consciousness weakened. Philosophical rationalism, the Enlightenment, the achievements of modern science, and the growth of materialism produced a worldview in which the Source could be disregarded altogether (see Appendix 1). This history helps explain why alignment research operates almost exclusively within the horizontal dimension.

If AI requires guidance from beyond its own reasoning, the same may be true of humanity. **Divine Alignment introduces the possibility of reversing this historical trajectory.** If reliable alignment requires guidance beyond human preferences and judgments, the search for a solution could lead us back toward the Source that humanity has progressively marginalized. Such a return would not restore the intuitive religious consciousness of ancient times. It could occur at a new level, through a conscious understanding of the relationship between intelligence, morality, and the foundation of existence. **The very achievements of reason and technology that contributed to the marginalization of the Source could become instruments of humanity's return to it.** In seeking to align artificial intelligence with divine instruction, humanity might rediscover the necessity of aligning itself with the same Source. Remarkably, this possibility resonates with an ancient prophetic vision.

## The Prophetic Horizon: Humanity's Return to God

Thousands of years ago, **the Hebrew prophets described a future in which humanity would undergo a profound spiritual transformation**, rediscovering its connection to the Creator and recognizing God as the ultimate Source of truth, morality, and guidance (see Appendix 4). Isaiah declares:

> _“For the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the Lord, as the waters cover the sea.”_ (Isaiah 11:9; cf. Habakkuk 2:14)

And Zechariah proclaims:

> _“And the Lord shall be King over all the earth; on that day the Lord shall be One and His name One.”_ (Zechariah 14:9)

These prophecies envision a world in which the recognition of God is no longer confined to particular nations or traditions, but becomes a universal dimension of human consciousness (ref_4). They describe more than acknowledgment; they envision **a profound transformation of human values, priorities, and conduct.** Zephaniah (3:9) foresees the nations united in serving God, and Isaiah (2:4) a world in which nations abandon warfare and learn peace. This vision has remained alive in Jewish consciousness through centuries of exile and persecution, and every day the prayer _Aleinu_ expresses the hope that all humanity will recognize God's sovereignty (ref_5). The prophets portray this return as humanity's destined future.

Viewed against this horizon, superintelligent AI acquires an unexpected significance. By attempting to create an intelligence greater than its own, humanity is compelled to confront questions that centuries of scientific progress had pushed aside: What is the ultimate foundation of morality? Where do human values originate? Can intelligence, however advanced, determine what is truly good without reference to a higher Source?

We began by asking whether AI alignment faces a situation like that of physics at the turn of the twentieth century. The answer may be that, as in physics, the way forward lies in questioning the framework itself and recovering the dimension it left out. **The very culmination of human intellectual achievement may become the occasion for rediscovering the Source from which humanity has gradually distanced itself.**
